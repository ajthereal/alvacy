The most common approaches

Six paths, and what the data says.

Each has its place. Tap through one by one to see what it does, what research says, and what's often missing.

Exercise

Running, gym, classes, anything that gets your body moving.

What it does
Burns energy and protects your heart and muscles. Almost everyone is advised to do it.
What's often missing
Exercise doesn't control what you eat, and the number on the scale moves only a little. That's why many feel “I've been exercising diligently but my body hasn't changed”.

What research says

−1.0 kg

extra weight loss when exercise is added to a diet, compared with diet alone. Exercise still improves heart risk factors even when weight doesn't drop. Source 8

Intermittent fasting (IF)

Eat only within a set eating window, for example 16:8.

What it does
Controls when you eat, so total calories automatically drop.
What's often missing
The eating window controls when, not what or how much. Protein and muscle aren't automatically protected, and lean mass loss is in line with an ordinary calorie-cutting diet.

What research says · 139 people, 12 months

Eating window + fewer calories−8.0 kg
Fewer calories only−6.3 kg

A difference of 1.8 kg, not statistically significant. Source 6

OMAD

All calories in one meal a day.

What it does
Cuts total intake through a very narrow eating window.
What's often missing
The data is still small and short-term, in healthy people of normal weight. There is no long-term evidence yet that this is safe and effective for most people.
My experience
I did OMAD. I lost weight, but it came back very easily.

What research says · small studies

  • 8 weeks, 15 people, calories matched. Body fat dropped about 2 kg more, but hunger was higher throughout the study, and blood pressure and LDL cholesterol rose.
  • 11 days, 11 people. Lean mass and bone mass fell more too.

Source 7, from a summary, not the original paper.

Nutrition doctor

Consultation and meal plan.

What it does
Builds a meal plan tailored to your medical condition.
A good fit if
You have diabetes, high cholesterol, kidney problems, or another condition that needs clinical adjustment.
What's often missing
A good plan still has to be lived by you every day, between meetings and business trips.

Worth asking

  • What composition is measured besides weight?
  • Who accompanies you every day, not every month?
  • What happens after the consultation ends?

Aesthetic doctors and slimming clinics

Programs and procedures under medical supervision.

What it does
Check-ups, programs, and sometimes procedures such as medication, injections, or devices. What's included differs from clinic to clinic.
A good fit if
You want a thorough evaluation, or need a procedure that only medical staff can perform.
What's often missing
The procedure works at the clinic, but habits are built at home.

What's really changing

What ages is what's inside your body.

“Body age” isn't a marketing term. Body composition monitors like Omron calculate it from your resting metabolic rate (RMR), as a guide to metabolic age. Source 11

Myth first
20–50s

Metabolism doesn't suddenly slow at 30. Data from more than 6,600 people in 29 countries shows daily energy expenditure stays fairly stable from your 20s to your 50s. It only declines about 0.7% a year after age 60. Source 9

What shifts
0.4–0.5% / year

Muscle mass in people over 65 is lower than in young adults, on average 0.47% per year in men and 0.37% in women. Strength falls 2 to 5 times faster than mass. Source 10

Why it matters
60–70%

Around 60 to 70% of our daily energy goes to basal metabolism, and muscle is one of its biggest contributors. Shrinking muscle makes your body's “engine” smaller. Sources 11 and 13

Same weight, different content

Two people can both weigh 70 kg on the scale. One has more fat and less muscle. The scale can't tell the difference.

Enough protein

Train your muscle

Resistance training at least 3 times a week, plus 150 minutes of moderate aerobic activity per week.

Measure body composition

Not just the scale. A bioimpedance device (body composition scan) is practical for tracking muscle repeatedly, and it's what we use in this program.

FAQ

Before you ask.

Is TRMe an anti-aging drug?

No. TRMe is a weight management program from Nu Skin made up of supplements, an eating pattern, and activity. What I explain on this page is aging of body composition, meaning muscle and fat, which can be measured. I don't claim TRMe reverses aging or treats disease.

I'm on or planning to use GLP-1. Is this still a fit?

The decision about medication is yours and your doctor's. What I can help with is the habits side: protein, movement, and measuring body composition. Expert guidelines also recommend enough protein and resistance training alongside this therapy. As for the medication, follow your doctor's guidance. Ask me on WhatsApp

Is this a replacement for a doctor or nutrition doctor?

No. I'm a coach, not medical staff. If you have a medical condition, are pregnant, or take regular medication, start with your doctor. The two can run side by side.

Why should I trust this?

You don't have to just believe it. Look at the real numbers and photos above, including the other coaches' results. I'm also upfront that I'm a Diamond Leader at Nu Skin and that the TRMe study I mention is an internal Nu Skin study. Measure first, then change, so your progress shows in data.
