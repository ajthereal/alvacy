A calorie deficit alone doesn’t fix your metabolism.

The numbers above are energy math. If your metabolism isn’t working well, eating less can make your body save energy, and what you lose is muscle. The result: the scale gets stuck, then the weight comes back.

  • Losing muscle lowers how much you burn every day.
  • Short sleep and stress make you hungrier and make fat easier to store.
  • Blood sugar swings leave you drained, sleepy, and craving sweets.

So before you start a deficit, talk to a coach to find out what’s really holding your progress back.

How daily calorie needs are calculated

This calculator uses the same three steps nutritionists use:

  1. Resting calories (BMR), the energy your body uses at rest, from the Mifflin-St Jeor equation: 10 × weight + 6.25 × height − 5 × age, then +5 for men or −161 for women.
  2. Daily needs (TDEE) = BMR times an activity factor, from 1.2 (mostly sitting) to 1.725 (very active).
  3. Your calories = daily needs minus a deficit. Losing 0.5 kg a week takes a deficit of about 550 kcal a day.

Why not lose weight faster

With too big a deficit your body burns muscle too, you feel hungry and drained, and the weight tends to come back. So this calculator caps the pace at 1% of body weight per week and never goes below 1,200 kcal (women) or 1,500 kcal (men).

Why the timeline slows down at the end

The lighter you get, the less energy you burn. At the same pace, the last kilos take longer. This calculator recalculates your needs every week, so the date is more realistic than a straight-line estimate.

Common questions

How many calories to lose 1 kg?

The common rule of thumb is a deficit of about 7,700 kcal per kg of fat. It’s an estimate. Early on, weight often drops faster because of water, then slows.

Do I have to count calories every day?

No. The number is a guide. Many people do fine just knowing their portions and food choices, for example with the Padang builder and protein target in our tools.

Is 1,200 kcal a day safe?

For many adult women, 1,200 kcal is the commonly used minimum. Below that it’s hard to meet your nutrient needs. This calculator never suggests less than that.

Why does protein matter when dieting?

Protein helps keep muscle while you lose weight and keeps you full longer. We use 1.6 g per kg of goal weight per day.

Who shouldn’t use this calculator?

People who are pregnant or breastfeeding, anyone under 18, and people with medical conditions like diabetes or kidney disease should talk to a doctor or dietitian first.

Where do the numbers come from?
  • Mifflin et al., 1990 (American Journal of Clinical Nutrition) · the resting-calorie equation this calculator uses
  • NIH Body Weight Planner · why weight loss slows over time
